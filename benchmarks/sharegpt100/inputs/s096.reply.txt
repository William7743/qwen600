The nationalists who met in 1786 to discuss issues regarding the United States government were the Annapolis Convention, also known as the Annapolis Convention of 1786. The convention was called to discuss issues related to commerce and trade among the states, but it also addressed broader questions about the weaknesses of the Articles of Confederation, the then-governing document of the United States. The Annapolis Convention was attended by representatives from five states: New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware, and Virginia. Although the convention did not achieve its stated goals, it is considered an important precursor to the Constitutional Convention, which was held in 1787 and resulted in the drafting of the United States Constitution.
